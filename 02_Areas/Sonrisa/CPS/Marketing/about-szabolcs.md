## 14. Voice & tone notes

Synthesized from the three published LinkedIn pieces (`#1`, `#2`, `#3` LinkedIn_v1.md).

**Core moves that appear in all three:**
- **First-person, observational, story-first.** Openers: "Something I see way too often:" / "Most companies never actually decide..." / "You budget EUR 70,000 for a DevOps engineer."
- **Show your evidence base.** "I have had this conversation with probably 50+ companies at this point." The voice has weight because it claims pattern-level seeing, not theory.
- **Refuse to push a side.** All three pieces explicitly say some version of: "I am not writing this to push anyone toward a managed service." The honest-broker register is the brand.
- **Concrete patterns over abstractions.** "Terraform state file, pipeline errors, backend is down." / "EUR 70,000 → EUR 150,000." / "Three months before they are actually productive." Specifics beat adjectives.
- **Short paragraphs, lots of white space.** Mobile-first reading. Two to four sentences per paragraph max.
- **Single clean handoff, not a CTA stack.** One link, one line: "Full article here:" / "Read the full article here:"

**Mechanical rules:**
- **No em dashes** (carries the CPS working-memory rule). Use en-dashes, commas, or colons.
- **British spelling.** "stabilise", "organisation", "optimisation" — consistent across the trilogy.
- **No buzzwords.** Avoid "digital transformation," "operational excellence," "synergies," "leverage."
- **No superlatives about CPS or yourself.** The voice is humble-by-design and matches CPS value #1: Alázat.
