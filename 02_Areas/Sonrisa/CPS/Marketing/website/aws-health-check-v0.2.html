Title Content Image
Idle Workloads
Unnecessary AWS capacity consuming budget.
  • Resources are provisioned for peak usage but remain active long after demand has dropped.
     
  • Teams often forget to shut down non-production environments, experiments, or temporary systems.
     
  • Over time, these "always-on" resources quietly consume budget without contributing to growth or reliability.
Oversized Resources
Cloud infrastructure larger than actual demand.
  • Systems are sized conservatively to avoid risk, but rarely revisited once in production.
     
  • Larger-than-necessary instances increase costs without improving customer experience.
     
  • Rightsizing is postponed because it feels risky, even when data shows it is safe.
Zombie Assets
Unused cloud assets still appearing on your bill.
  • Storage volumes, snapshots, and backups remain after projects end or teams change.
     
  • These assets are rarely visible in day-to-day operations but continue to generate costs.
     
  • Without regular cleanup, they accumulate and become impossible to trace back to a business decision.
Tagging Chaos
Lack of clear cost allocation and visibility.
  • Inconsistent or missing tagging makes it unclear which team, product, or initiative owns which costs.
     
  • Finance sees growing spend, while engineering lacks actionable visibility.
     
  • This prevents accountability, chargebacks, and informed prioritization at leadership level.

For finance and leadership teams, this creates unpredictable AWS cloud spend, poor cost visibility, and a lack of accountability across teams and products.
