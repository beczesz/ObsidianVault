Something I see way too often:

A company hires their first DevOps engineer, things stabilise, everyone relaxes. Then that person goes on holiday. Or hands in their notice.

Suddenly nobody knows what the Terraform state file is, the pipeline is throwing errors nobody has seen before, and the backend is down.

The engineer was great. The problem was never the engineer. The problem is that modern cloud infrastructure is simply too broad for one person to cover sustainably. Security, CI/CD, Kubernetes, FinOps, compliance - that is not one job, that is five.

We have seen this pattern at companies of all sizes. And in almost every case, they knew something was fragile. They just hoped it would hold a little longer.

It usually does. Until it does not.

I wrote about the four structural risks of the single-engineer model and what a resilient DevOps function actually requires. No sales pitch - if hiring internally is the right answer for your situation, I will tell you that too.
