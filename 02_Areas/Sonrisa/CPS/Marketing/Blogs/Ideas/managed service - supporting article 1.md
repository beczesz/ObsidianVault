### Section 2: The Risks You Don't See Until It's Too Late (300 words)

**Key points:**
- **Knowledge silos:** Undocumented runbooks, tribal knowledge, "only Janos knows how the deployment works"
- **Burnout and turnover:** On-call fatigue, scope creep, being the "infrastructure firefighter" 24/7
- **Vacation and sick leave:** Who handles the 3 AM alert when your one DevOps person is unavailable?
- **Career stagnation:** A solo DevOps engineer has no peers to learn from — they plateau, then leave
- **BMC Connection — Customer Pain Points:**
  - "Our DevOps guy left and we're stuck"
  - "We can't deploy because nobody understands the pipeline"
  - "We had a 6-hour outage because the one person who knew the system was sick"

**Supporting data/examples:**
- Average tenure of a DevOps engineer (industry data: ~2 years)
- Cost of a single serious outage vs. monthly managed service cost
- The "lottery factor" — what if your key person wins the lottery tomorrow?

## Notes for Writing

- **Tone:** Empathetic, not fear-mongering. Acknowledge that the single-engineer setup is normal and common — it's a growth stage, not a failure.
- **Voice:** Speak from experience ("We've seen this pattern dozens of times...")
- **Key CPS values to weave in:** Alázat (humility — we don't judge, we help), Proaktivitás (prevention over reaction), Személyes kapcsolatok (personal relationships — we understand your situation)
- **Avoid:** Making the reader feel bad about their current setup. Instead, show the path forward.
