## Section 1: Two Different Bets (150 words)

**Key points:**
- **Hiring in-house** is a bet on finding and retaining the right people over time
- **Managed service** is a bet on operational continuity regardless of who's on your payroll
- Neither is inherently better — they solve different problems
- The question to ask yourself: *"What is more dangerous for us right now — people risk or operational risk?"*

**People risk:** You're one resignation away from infrastructure paralysis
**Operational risk:** A third party doesn't know your systems as well as someone inside

- **Bridge:** Both risks are real. The framework below helps you figure out which one you should prioritize.

## Section 2: The Decision Framework (300 words)

**Intro line:** Five questions. Answer them honestly — they'll tell you where to go.

**Question 1: What stage is your company at?**
- Startup / scale-up under 50 people: Managed service almost always wins — you need breadth, not headcount
- 50-200 people, growing fast: Managed service or hybrid — you need capacity now, not in 6 months
- 200-500 people, stable: Consider in-house with managed support for coverage and architecture
- 500+ people, DevOps as core competency: In-house team with managed service as specialist backup

**Question 2: Do you need more than 2-3 cloud/infra specialties?**
- AWS + GCP + Kubernetes + Terraform + security + cost optimization = you need a team, not a person
- One hire realistically covers 2-3 of these well
- Managed service: you get the whole team under one contract

**Question 3: How fast do you need this?**
- Time to hire senior DevOps: 3-6 months (plus 1-3 months ramp-up)
- Time to onboard with a managed service: 2-4 weeks
- If you have a problem now, hiring is not the solution now

**Question 4: What happens when your DevOps person is unavailable?**
- Vacation, sick leave, resignation — is there a backup?
- If the answer is "we'd be stuck," that's your answer
- Managed service provides team continuity by design

**Question 5: Is DevOps a core competency of your product?**
- Yes (infrastructure IS your product): In-house, full-time team
- No (infrastructure supports your product): Managed service or hybrid

## Section 3: When Each Option Wins — Honest Summary (200 words)

**A clean comparison — no spin:**

| | In-House | Managed Service | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best for** | 200+ team, DevOps-core | 5-200 team, scaling fast | 50-500, 1-2 internal engineers |
| **Time to capability** | 4-9 months | 2-4 weeks | Immediate for managed scope |
| **Cost** | €100k-€158k/year (true cost) | €24k-€72k/year | Variable |
| **Breadth** | 2-3 specialties | Full team | Combined |
| **Continuity** | Single point of failure | Team by design | Shared |

**The hybrid model (underrated):**
- Your internal engineer handles day-to-day context and developer relations
- Managed service covers: architecture, breadth, backup, FinOps, security
- This is how many CPS clients operate — and it's often the most practical path
- Example: 1 internal engineer + CPS Essential (40h/month) = full coverage at a fraction of the cost of building a team
