# You Budgeted €50,000 for a DevOps Engineer. Here Is Why It Costs €100,000.

> **Series:** Managed Service Series - Article 3 of 3
> **Reading time:** ~6 minutes
> **Target:** CFOs, CTOs, VP Engineering evaluating budget allocation

---

The number on the offer letter is not the number you will pay.

Every company that has hired a senior DevOps engineer has learned this the hard way. The salary is visible. The employer contributions, the recruitment agency fees, the two to three months before they are fully productive, the tooling licenses, the training budget, the management time -- none of these are unusual. None of them are in the original budget.

The result is a predictable gap: you budget €50,000-60,000, and by the end of year one you have spent €90,000-120,000 or more. Not because anyone made a mistake. Because that is what a senior technical hire actually costs when you count everything.

## The True Cost of One DevOps Engineer: Year One

The table below uses Central European market rates (Romania, Hungary, Poland). Ranges reflect real variation across the region.

| Cost Category | Estimated Annual Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Base salary | €35,000-70,000 | Senior level; Romania/Hungary lower end, Poland higher |
| Employer contributions | €4,000-15,000 | Varies significantly: Romania ~8%, Hungary ~15%, Poland ~21% |
| Recruitment cost | €8,000-15,000 | Agency fee (typically 15-20% of annual salary) |
| Onboarding and ramp-up | €4,000-8,000 | 2-3 months to full productivity |
| Tooling and licenses | €2,000-6,000 | Monitoring, CI/CD, security tooling |
| Training and certifications | €1,000-4,000 | AWS, Kubernetes, security credentials |
| Management overhead | €3,000-8,000 | 1:1s, reviews, integration, context-sharing |
| **Total Year 1** | **€65,000-120,000** | **vs. €35,000-70,000 budgeted salary** |

The gap between what you think you are paying and what you are actually paying is consistently 1.5 to 2x the base salary. In every country, at every level.

## Two Things That Make It Worse Over Time

**The turnover multiplier.** According to industry data, roughly 45% of software engineers stay in a role for 1 to 2 years -- and in the DevOps space, where demand outpaces supply, the number is similar. ([Source: Spacelift DevOps Statistics](https://spacelift.io/blog/devops-statistics)) That means you pay the recruitment and onboarding costs above on a roughly 2-year cycle. Every time your engineer leaves, you pay the ramp cost again -- and carry the knowledge loss on top.

**The vacancy gap.** Average time to hire a senior DevOps engineer in Central Europe is 3-5 months, with over 60% of companies struggling to find qualified candidates within that window. During the gap, someone covers the role who was not hired for it, or no one does, or you pay a contractor at emergency day rates. In most cases, all three happen in sequence.

## The Cost Nobody Calculates: What Happens When Things Break

Beyond the salary stack, there is a cost category that does not appear in any headcount model: what happens when things go wrong.

For mid-sized companies, unplanned downtime typically costs €5,000 to €50,000 per hour depending on business type and revenue model. A single serious incident -- a misconfigured auto-scaling rule, a failed production deployment, an undetected security gap -- can cost more than months of managed service.

Two scenarios we see regularly:

**Scenario 1 -- The unexpected cloud bill.** An auto-scaling rule without a cap generates €15,000 in compute costs in a single month. A FinOps review at €500/month would have flagged the configuration within the first week.

**Scenario 2 -- The weekend outage.** A production failure on a Saturday morning takes four hours to resolve because the on-call engineer cannot be reached. Lost revenue: €20,000. 24/7 on-call coverage reduces mean time to resolution from hours to minutes.

In both cases, the preventive cost is a fraction of the damage. **Proactive management is not an expense. It is insurance that pays for itself.**
