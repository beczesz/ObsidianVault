# You Budgeted €70,000 for a DevOps Engineer. Here Is Why It Costs €150,000.

**Series:** Managed Service Series - Article 3 of 3
**Reading time:** ~6 minutes
**Target:** CFOs, CTOs, VP Engineering evaluating budget allocation

---

The number on the offer letter is not the number you will pay.

Every company that has hired a senior DevOps engineer has learned this the hard way. The salary is visible. The employer taxes, the recruitment agency fees, the three months before they are productive, the tooling licenses, the annual training budget, the management time -- those costs are real, they are significant, and almost nobody adds them up before making the hire.

The result is a predictable gap: you budget EUR 70,000, and by the end of year one you have spent EUR 110,000 to EUR 158,000. Not because anyone made a mistake. Because that is what a senior technical hire actually costs when you count everything.

This article lays out the full picture -- for both in-house hiring and managed services -- so you can make the decision based on real numbers.

---

## The True Cost of One DevOps Engineer: Year One

The table below uses EU market rates. The ranges reflect variation across Central and Western Europe.

| Cost Category | Estimated Annual Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Base salary | EUR 60,000-80,000 | Senior level, EU market |
| Employer taxes and benefits | EUR 15,000-25,000 | ~25-35% above salary depending on country |
| Recruitment cost | EUR 10,000-20,000 | Agency fee or internal HR time at market rate |
| Onboarding and ramp-up | EUR 5,000-10,000 | 2-3 months before full productivity |
| Tooling and licenses | EUR 3,000-8,000 | Monitoring, CI/CD, security tooling |
| Training and certifications | EUR 2,000-5,000 | AWS, Kubernetes, security credentials |
| Management overhead | EUR 5,000-10,000 | 1:1s, reviews, integration, context-sharing |
| **Total Year 1** | **EUR 100,000-158,000** | **vs. EUR 70,000 budgeted salary** |

That gap -- between what you think you are paying and what you are actually paying -- is not an edge case. It is the norm. The fully loaded cost of a technical hire is consistently 1.5 to 2x the base salary.

---

## Two Things That Make It Worse Over Time

The year one figure is not the ceiling. Two factors compound the cost.

**The turnover multiplier.** The average DevOps engineer stays in a role for roughly two years. That means the recruitment and onboarding costs above repeat on a roughly 24-month cycle. Every time your engineer leaves, you pay the ramp cost again -- and carry the knowledge loss on top.

**The vacancy gap.** The average time to hire a senior DevOps engineer is 3-6 months. During that gap, someone is covering the role who was not hired to do it, or no one is, or you are paying a contractor at emergency rates. In most cases, all three happen in sequence. That gap has a cost that almost nobody budgets for in advance.

## The Cost Nobody Calculates: What Happens When Things Break

Beyond the salary stack, there is a cost category that does not appear in any headcount model: the cost of incidents, downtime, and reactive response.

For mid-sized companies, downtime typically costs EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 per hour, depending on business type and revenue model. A single serious incident -- a misconfigured auto-scaling rule, a failed deployment to production, an undetected security gap -- can cost more than an entire year of managed service.

Two scenarios we see regularly:

**Scenario 1 -- The unexpected cloud bill.** An auto-scaling rule without a cap generates EUR 15,000 in compute costs in a single month. A FinOps review, available as a EUR 500/month add-on, would have flagged the configuration within the first week.

**Scenario 2 -- The weekend outage.** A production failure on a Saturday morning takes four hours to resolve because the on-call engineer cannot be reached and no one else has access. Lost revenue: EUR 20,000. 24/7 on-call support at EUR 2,000/month reduces mean time to resolution from hours to minutes.

In both scenarios, the preventive cost is a fraction of the damage. Proactive management is not a budget line item. It is insurance that pays for itself.

## A Note on the Hybrid Path

The most underrated option in this comparison is the hybrid model: one internal engineer handling context and developer relations, combined with a managed service covering architecture, specialist depth, on-call rotation, and proactive monitoring.

For companies in the 50-500 employee range, this typically delivers better coverage than a solo in-house hire and significantly lower cost than a full in-house team. It is more common than the either/or framing suggests.
