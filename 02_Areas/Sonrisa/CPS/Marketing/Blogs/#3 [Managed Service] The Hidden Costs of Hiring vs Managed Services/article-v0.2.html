The Number on the Offer Letter Is Not the Number You Will Pay

Every company that has hired a senior DevOps engineer has learned this the hard way. The salary is visible. The employer taxes, the recruitment agency fees, the three months before they are productive, the tooling licenses, the annual training budget, the management time - those costs are real, they are significant, and almost nobody adds them up before making the hire.

The result is a predictable gap: you budget EUR 70,000, and by the end of year one you have spent EUR 110,000 to EUR 158,000. Not because anyone made a mistake. Because that is what a senior technical hire actually costs when you count everything.

The fully loaded cost of a technical hire is consistently 1.5 to 2x the base salary. This is not an edge case - it is the norm.

 

The Turnover Multiplier

 

The average DevOps engineer stays in a role for roughly two years. That means the recruitment and onboarding costs above repeat on a roughly 24-month cycle. Every time your engineer leaves, you pay the ramp cost again - and carry the knowledge loss on top.
The Vacancy Gap

 

Average time to hire a senior DevOps engineer: 3-6 months. During that gap, someone is covering the role who was not hired to do it, or no one is, or you are paying a contractor at emergency rates. In most cases, all three happen in sequence.

 

 

The Cost Nobody Calculates: What Happens When Things Break

Beyond the salary stack, there is a cost category that does not appear in any headcount model.
The cost of prevention is always lower than the cost of recovery.

 

The Unexpected Cloud Bill

 

An auto-scaling rule without a cap generates EUR 15,000 in compute costs in a single month.

 

Prevention: A FinOps review at EUR 500/month would have flagged the configuration within the first week.
The Weekend Outage

 

A production failure on a Saturday morning takes four hours to resolve because the on-call engineer cannot be reached. Lost revenue: EUR 20,000.

 

Prevention: 24/7 on-call support at EUR 2,000/month reduces mean time to resolution from hours to minutes.

 

Downtime costs EUR 5,000-50,000 per hour for mid-sized companies.
A single incident can cost more than a full year of managed service.
