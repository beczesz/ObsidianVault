the Predictability of Managed Services

A DevOps engineer costs far more than their salary. We break down the real numbers and compare them to predictable managed service pricing.

 

 

Where the Money Actually Goes

You budget EUR 70,000 for a DevOps engineer. By the end of year one, you have spent EUR 110,000 or more.

Salary is the tip of the iceberg. Recruitment, onboarding, tooling, training, and unplanned downtime are the hidden mass below the surface. Most companies only see the full picture in hindsight.

Managed services are not cheaper because they cut corners. They are more predictable because all costs are visible upfront.

The Turnover Multiplier
Average DevOps tenure is roughly two years. That means you pay this ramp-up cost every 24 months - recruitment, onboarding, knowledge transfer, all over again.
The Vacancy Gap
Average time to hire a senior DevOps engineer: 3-6 months. Who handles the incidents, the deployments, and the security patches during that gap?

 

 

The Cost Nobody Calculates - Downtime and Incidents

Beyond salary, there is a cost most companies only recognise after the fact.
The cost of prevention is always lower than the cost of recovery.

 

The Surprise Cloud Bill
A misconfigured auto-scaling rule leads to a EUR 15,000 cloud bill in a single month.
Prevention: A FinOps review (EUR 500/month) would have caught it within the first week.
The Weekend Outage
A 4-hour outage during business hours costs EUR 20,000 in lost revenue.
Prevention: 24/7 on-call support (EUR 2,000/month) reduces resolution time from hours to minutes.

 

Downtime costs EUR 5,000-50,000 per hour.
A single incident can cost more than a full year of managed service.
