# Managed DevOps vs. Hiring In-House: How to Decide

**Series:** Managed Service Series - Article 2 of 3
**Reading time:** ~5 minutes
**Target:** CTOs, VP Engineering, IT Directors (20-500 employees)

---

Most companies do not decide between managed DevOps and in-house. They drift into one or the other. Someone gets hired because a deployment broke. A vendor gets called because nobody else was available. Then, six months later, leadership realises the "decision" was never actually made.

This article gives you a framework to make that decision consciously -- based on where your company is right now, not where you hope to be.

We have helped over 50 companies navigate this. Sometimes the answer is managed service. Sometimes it is hiring. Sometimes it is both.

---

## Two Different Bets

This is not a technical choice. It is a strategic one about where your infrastructure risk sits.

**Hiring in-house** is a bet on finding and retaining the right people over time. You get deep context, cultural alignment, and someone who knows every workaround in your codebase. The risk: you are one resignation away from infrastructure paralysis.

**A managed service** is a bet on operational continuity regardless of who is on your payroll. You get breadth, coverage, and team-level resilience from day one. The risk: a third party will never know your systems as intimately as someone who built them.

Neither is inherently better. They solve different problems. The question to ask yourself: **what is more dangerous for us right now -- people risk or operational risk?**

## Five Questions That Tell You Where to Go

Answer them honestly. They will point you in the right direction.

### 1. What stage is your company at?

| Company Stage | Typical Best Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Startup / scale-up (<50 people)** | Managed service | You need breadth, not headcount |
| **Growing fast (50-200 people)** | Managed or hybrid | You need capacity now, not in 6 months |
| **Established (200-500 people)** | In-house + managed support | Coverage and architecture review |
| **Large (500+ people)** | In-house team | Managed service as specialist backup |

### 2. Do you need more than 2-3 specialties?

AWS, GCP, Kubernetes, Terraform, security, cost optimisation -- that is six different disciplines. One hire realistically covers two or three of them well. If your infrastructure spans more than that, you need a team, not a person. A managed service gives you the whole team under one contract.

### 3. How fast do you need this?

Time to hire a senior DevOps engineer: 3-6 months, plus 1-3 months of ramp-up. Time to onboard with a managed service: 2-4 weeks. If you have a problem right now, hiring is not the solution right now.

### 4. What happens when your DevOps person is unavailable?

Holiday, sick leave, resignation -- is there a backup? If the honest answer is "we would figure it out somehow," that is not a plan. That is hope. A managed service provides team continuity by design.

### 5. Is DevOps a core competency of your product?

If infrastructure IS your product -- you are a hosting company, a cloud provider, a platform business -- build an in-house team. Full stop. But if infrastructure supports your product, keeping it fully in-house may not be the best use of your budget or management attention.

## When Each Option Wins -- An Honest Summary

| | In-House | Managed Service | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best for** | 200+ team, DevOps is core | 5-200 team, scaling fast | 50-500, 1-2 internal engineers |
| **Time to capability** | 4-9 months | 2-4 weeks | Immediate for managed scope |
| **Annual cost** | EUR 100,000-158,000 (true cost) | EUR 24,000-72,000 | Variable |
| **Breadth of expertise** | 2-3 specialties per hire | Full team coverage | Combined |
| **Continuity risk** | Single point of failure | Team by design | Shared |

**The hybrid model is underrated.** Your internal engineer handles day-to-day context and developer relations. The managed service covers architecture, breadth, backup, FinOps, and security. This is how many of our clients operate -- and it is often the most practical path.

Example: one internal engineer plus a managed service package (40 hours/month) gives you full coverage at a fraction of the cost of building a three-person team.

## What We Have Learned From 50+ Companies

A few patterns we see again and again:

**Most companies wait too long.** They bring in a managed service after an incident, not before. The companies that thrive operationally plan for redundancy before they need it.

**The best partnerships are not vendor relationships.** They are built on trust and honest communication. When we tell a client they do not need us, that builds more trust than any sales pitch ever could.

**We turn away clients who do not need us.** If in-house is the better answer for your situation, we will tell you. Winning for both sides is the only model that works long-term.

**The hybrid model is more common than people think.** And it is often the best answer for companies in the 50-500 employee range.

---

## Make the Decision Before the Incident

The goal is not to pick managed service or in-house. The goal is to stop being one resignation or one outage away from a crisis.

If you have read this far, you probably already know which direction makes sense. The next step is a conversation -- not a commitment.
