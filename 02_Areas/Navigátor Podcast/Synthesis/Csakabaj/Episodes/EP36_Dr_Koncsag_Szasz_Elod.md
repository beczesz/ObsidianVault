## Information Architecture (Key Knowledge Points)

### On Fever

**Central Insight:**
Fever itself is rarely dangerous (with exceptions). Fever is body's immune response. Most fevers under 39°C are process, not pathology.

**Parent Anxiety Source:**
- Cultural messaging treats fever as dangerous
- History of fever causing seizures (rare modern occurrence)
- Pharmaceutical industry marketing (fever-reducers)
- Uncertainty creates anxiety

**Dr. Előd's Correction:**
- Fever is helpful immune process
- Focus on child's behavior, not number on thermometer
- Most fevers resolve without intervention
- Reducing fever doesn't improve outcomes (except comfort)

### On Antibiotics

**Problem:**
- Overprescription creates antibiotic resistance
- Antibiotics harm normal bacterial flora
- Many infections are viral (antibiotics don't help)
- Pressure from anxious parents drives overuse

**Solution:**
- Distinguish bacterial vs. viral (testing helps)
- Tolerate uncertainty sometimes
- Trust observation of child
- Reduce unnecessary prescribing

### On Immunity

**Key Points:**
- Exposure to infection builds immunity
- Too-sterile environment harms long-term immunity
- Viral infections train immune system
- Vaccination trains immunity without risk of full disease

**Hungarian Context:**
- Healthcare system may have different vaccination schedule
- Cultural attitudes toward cleanliness/risk vary
- Access to care shapes decision-making

## Hosting Challenges: Interview Format in Medical Context

### Challenge 1: Authority and Expertise

Levi is not a doctor. How does he position himself?

**Options:**
- **Perform expertise** — Pretend to know. Bad. Damages credibility.
- **Excessive humility** — "I don't know anything." Boring. Doesn't serve audience.
- **Strategic ignorance** — "I'm confused about this like most parents are—help me understand." Good. Honest.

**Likely approach:** Levi represents audience anxiety and confusion. He's good at this.

### Challenge 2: Density Management

Medical information is dense. How does Levi maintain engagement?

**Strategies:**
- Ask questions when confused (audience will be confused too)
- Request practical examples ("What would you do if...?")
- Translate jargon into parent language
- Push back gently ("But don't we all worry that...")

This keeps conversation rather than making it lecture.

### Challenge 3: Emotion vs. Information

Parents bring emotion (anxiety, guilt, love) to health decisions. Dr. Előd brings data. How does Levi bridge?

**Approach:**
- Honor parent anxiety as real (even if statistically unfounded)
- Help translate data into parent language
- Acknowledge difficulty of uncertainty
- Avoid making parents feel stupid for their fears

### Challenge 4: Myth-Busting Without Condescension

Hungarian culture likely has health myths. Dr. Előd will bust them. How does Levi do this respectfully?

**Good approach:**
- "I believed this my whole life... so tell me why that's wrong"
- Name the myth as widespread (not person-specific)
- Explain the source (how did this belief form?)
- Provide counter-evidence gently
