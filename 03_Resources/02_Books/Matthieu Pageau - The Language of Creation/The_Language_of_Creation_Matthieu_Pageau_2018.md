# The Language of Creation — Matthieu Pageau (2018)

## Context

- **Author biography:** Matthieu Pageau is a Canadian author who lives in Quebec.  For more than a decade he has discussed religious philosophy and symbolism with his brother, icon‑carver and public thinker Jonathan Pageau.  These conversations inspired him to write his first book, *The Language of Creation: Cosmic Symbolism in Genesis*, a commentary that explores the symbolic language of the Bible【945957473681713†L21-L24】.  Pageau approaches the Bible as a coherent symbolic system rather than a collection of historical facts and writes from within the Eastern Orthodox tradition of allegorical interpretation.
- **Historical context:** Published in 2018, Pageau’s commentary responds to a contemporary crisis in which scientific and materialist worldviews have largely displaced the spiritual cosmology of the Bible.  He notes that as technological knowledge distances us from nature, people find it hard to reconcile modern science with the biblical worldview, and he seeks to equip readers with tools to interpret Genesis on its own terms【731753810066712†L195-L201】.  His work resonates with renewed interest in symbolism and myth among audiences influenced by thinkers such as Jordan Peterson.
- **Genre & tradition:** The book is a work of biblical commentary and symbolic theology.  It draws on traditional cosmology, patristic exegesis and modern symbolic analysis to reinterpret the early chapters of Genesis.  Rather than offering linguistic or historical criticism, Pageau treats Genesis as a structured symbolic narrative whose cosmic dualities (heaven/earth, time/space) mirror human experience.
- **Contrarian signal:** Pageau argues that modern materialism fails to capture the deeper meaning of reality.  He contrasts the scientific tendency to describe phenomena in terms of atoms and causality with the spiritual worldview that seeks higher meaning and interprets the physical world as the manifestation of metaphysical truth【731753810066712†L226-L239】.  By rejecting attempts to reconcile the two, he advocates rediscovering the lost spiritual worldview and interpreting the Bible accordingly【731753810066712†L254-L259】.
- **Reception & influence:** Although niche, *The Language of Creation* gained popularity among listeners of symbolic podcasts and readers of Jordan Peterson’s works.  Pageau has been invited to discuss his ideas on platforms such as *The Jordan B. Peterson Podcast*, where he was introduced as a Canadian author living in Quebec who spent years exploring religious symbolism with his brother【945957473681713†L21-L24】.  The book reached high rankings on Amazon’s religious commentary lists and is often cited in online discussions about symbolic interpretation of Scripture.
- **Comparable works:** Pageau’s approach echoes earlier symbolic commentaries like James B. Jordan’s *Through New Eyes* and Father Alexander Schmemann’s *For the Life of the World*.  It can be contrasted with scientific‑historical commentaries on Genesis (e.g., John Walton’s *The Lost World of Genesis One*) that seek to reconcile ancient cosmology with modern science.
- **Who should read:** Readers interested in biblical symbolism, theology, Eastern Orthodox exegesis or Jordan Peterson’s mythological analysis will find this book useful.  It requires patience and openness to metaphorical thinking rather than literalism.

## Abstract

*The Language of Creation: Cosmic Symbolism in Genesis* proposes that the Bible conveys meaning through a symbolic “language of creation” grounded in the duality of heaven and earth.  Pageau argues that modern scientific frameworks describe reality solely in material terms and consequently miss the Bible’s metaphysical dimension【731753810066712†L226-L239】.  He writes to provide readers with tools to interpret Genesis from its own cosmological perspective instead of through materialistic paradigms【731753810066712†L195-L201】.  The commentary opens by comparing scientific and traditional cosmologies and stresses the importance of rediscovering the ancient biblical worldview【731753810066712†L203-L214】.  It then examines the first days of creation and the cosmic duality of “heaven and earth,” applying this pattern to stories of Adam and Eve, Cain and Abel, and Noah【731753810066712†L206-L209】.  Later sections explore how the dualities of space and time underpin biblical symbolism and how human beings function as microcosms of the cosmos.  Pageau discusses examples such as the cherubim of Ezekiel, Nebuchadnezzar’s dreams and Adam’s role as mediator between heaven and earth.  The final part constructs a metacognitive model of the universe and shows how this symbolic language can illuminate Scripture.  The book contends that reality should be seen not as a heap of meaningless atoms but as a physical expression of metaphysical truth【731753810066712†L254-L259】.

## Chapter Outline (Deep Analysis)

### Introduction – Reconciling Science and Scripture

**Guiding question:** Why do modern readers struggle to understand the Bible’s cosmology?

**Expanded answer & key ideas:** Pageau opens by observing that technological progress has distanced people from the natural world and made it difficult to reconcile the scientific worldview with the biblical worldview.  He states that the goal of his commentary is to equip readers to interpret the Bible from its own cosmological perspective rather than through materialistic paradigms【731753810066712†L195-L201】.  He emphasises that traditional cosmologies view the world in terms of spiritual principles, whereas modern science views it through atoms, energy and causality【731753810066712†L226-L239】.  Pageau invites readers to rediscover a spiritual worldview where physical phenomena embody metaphysical truths.

**Thesis statement(s):** Modern materialism cannot explain the Bible’s symbolic cosmology; to interpret Genesis, one must recover the ancient language of creation.

**Commentary:** The introduction sets the stage by framing the commentary as a corrective to materialism.  Pageau’s definition of two worldviews underscores that his goal is not to synthesize science and religion but to rediscover a lost metaphysical perspective.  This resonates with readers who sense a gap between scientific explanations and existential meaning.

**Skeptical challenge:** Some may argue that symbol and history are not mutually exclusive, and that scientific cosmology does not preclude spiritual interpretation.  Others may question whether Pageau’s dichotomy oversimplifies complex traditions.

**Applications & implications:** Relearning the “language of creation” can enrich spiritual practice, deepen engagement with Scripture and offer a way to discuss meaning in a secular age.

### Part I – Salvaging Creation from the Scientific Worldview

**Guiding question:** What are the differences between scientific and traditional cosmologies?

**Expanded answer & key ideas:** Pageau contrasts modern science, which explains phenomena in terms of matter and causality, with traditional cosmologies that interpret the world in terms of higher meaning【731753810066712†L226-L239】.  He examines how the Copernican revolution and subsequent scientific discoveries transformed notions of space, time and causality, undermining the foundations of traditional metaphysics【731753810066712†L275-L281】.  This part culminates in a call to rediscover the spiritual worldview and interpret the Bible accordingly【731753810066712†L254-L259】.

**Thesis statement(s):** Scientific explanations have eclipsed traditional metaphysics, but they cannot answer questions of meaning; therefore a spiritual worldview must be restored.

**Commentary:** Pageau’s critique of the scientific worldview echoes concerns from theologians and philosophers about reductionism.  He frames scientific progress as both a technical achievement and a metaphysical crisis.  His appeal to premodern cosmologies provides context for later chapters.

**Skeptical challenge:** Critics may contend that the history of science is more complex and that early scientists were often deeply religious.  A binary opposition between science and spirituality can obscure nuanced interactions.

**Applications & implications:** Understanding the limits of scientific metaphors may encourage readers to integrate symbolic and spiritual approaches alongside scientific knowledge.

### Part II – Heaven and Earth in Biblical Cosmology

**Guiding question:** How does the duality of heaven and earth function as a foundational symbol?

**Expanded answer & key ideas:** Pageau argues that the phrase “heaven and earth” denotes a cosmic duality that recurs throughout Genesis.  He analyses this symbol from both spiritual and material perspectives, showing how ancient cultures interpreted reality through principles like angels, demons and cosmic monsters【731753810066712†L226-L239】.  Chapters examine how the universe operates as a language in which spiritual meaning is encoded.  Rules of symbolism are outlined, with examples such as the cherubim of Ezekiel and Nebuchadnezzar’s dream.  Light and darkness, spiritual and material dimensions are contrasted to reveal how symbols communicate metaphysical truths.

**Thesis statement(s):** “Heaven and earth” is the fundamental binary through which the Bible expresses cosmic order; understanding its symbolism unlocks the language of creation.

**Commentary:** This section translates ancient cosmological categories into symbolic language accessible to modern readers.  By treating the universe as a meaningful text, Pageau invites readers to perceive patterns across seemingly disparate stories.

**Skeptical challenge:** Some may question whether the duality of heaven and earth is universally applicable or unique to a particular cultural milieu.  Others may argue that Pageau reads too much into narrative details.

**Applications & implications:** Recognizing these symbols can deepen appreciation for biblical narratives and provide frameworks for interpreting myths and rituals across cultures.

### Part III – Heaven and Earth on the Human Scale

**Guiding question:** In what ways do human beings mirror cosmic dualities?

**Expanded answer & key ideas:** Pageau explores how humanity is created “in the image of God” and functions as a microcosm of the cosmos.  Chapters analyse Adam as both breath and body, a mediator who names animals and hosts angels.  Examples include manna and flesh, Abraham as mediator, sexuality as microcosm and Nebuchadnezzar’s dreams.  These stories illustrate how human actions reflect cosmic patterns.  The section emphasises the importance of mediating between heaven and earth through ritual, law and virtue.

**Thesis statement(s):** Humans embody the cosmic order; understanding our symbolic role allows us to mediate between spiritual and material realms.

**Commentary:** By grounding cosmic symbolism in the human body and social life, Pageau makes abstract concepts tangible.  His focus on mediation aligns with Eastern Orthodox anthropology, which views humanity as priestly and sacramental.

**Skeptical challenge:** The claim that all aspects of human life mirror cosmic dualities may seem speculative.  Interpretive caution is needed to avoid forcing symbolism onto details.

**Applications & implications:** This section encourages readers to see their lives as participating in a larger cosmic narrative, inspiring ethical reflection and ritual practice.

### Part IV – Time and Space in Biblical Cosmology

**Guiding question:** How are time and space symbolised in Genesis, and what do these symbols teach about the nature of reality?

**Expanded answer & key ideas:** Pageau examines how the concepts of time and space function as mediators of knowledge.  He analyses symbols such as pillars, axes, foundation stones, scandal stones, seals and ornaments.  He describes how time is portrayed as mixture versus purity, continuous versus discrete, absurd versus reasonable, irrational versus rational, and how the Creator is revealed and concealed through these patterns.  Examples include work and rest, tools and instruments, the Sabbath, Jubilee and the walls of Jericho.  This section shows that time and space are not neutral but convey moral and metaphysical meaning.

**Thesis statement(s):** Biblical symbols of time and space encode spiritual teachings about order, rest, movement and transformation.

**Commentary:** Pageau synthesises disparate symbols into a coherent framework, demonstrating the richness of the biblical imagination.  His detailed analysis may challenge readers unfamiliar with typological interpretation but offers a comprehensive symbolic grammar.

**Skeptical challenge:** Modern readers may find the proliferation of symbolic correspondences overwhelming or arbitrary.  Balancing creative insight with textual fidelity is essential.

**Applications & implications:** Understanding these symbols can inform liturgical practice (e.g., Sabbath observance) and provide metaphors for personal growth and social structures.

### Part V – Time and Space on the Human Scale

**Guiding question:** How do cosmic symbols of time and space manifest in human life and morality?

**Expanded answer & key ideas:** This section extends the previous analysis to human experience.  Topics include the Garden of Eden, the Tree of Knowledge, the definition of sin, eating forbidden fruit, nakedness and clothing, sleep and work, dreaming and wandering, trees and snakes as mediators of knowledge, miracles of Moses, sexuality, reproduction, taboo and cyclical time, covenants, wine and fermentation, and the descendants of Adam and Noah in relation to time and space.  Pageau shows how each symbolic pattern instructs humans about virtue, vice and our relationship to the divine.

**Thesis statement(s):** Human moral life reflects cosmic principles; by reading these symbols, we can discern ethical and spiritual lessons.

**Commentary:** Pageau’s emphasis on microcosm continues, illustrating how everyday actions and biological processes carry symbolic weight.  This holistic view integrates body, society and cosmos into a unified vision of creation.

**Skeptical challenge:** Some readers may question whether all these details are intended by the biblical authors or are later interpretive overlays.

**Applications & implications:** Recognising symbolism in daily life can inspire mindfulness, ethical deliberation and appreciation for ritual.

### Part VI – Transcending the Scientific Worldview

**Guiding question:** How can the language of creation offer a metacognitive model for understanding the universe?

**Expanded answer & key ideas:** In the final part, Pageau presents a metacognitive model that synthesises his symbolic analysis.  He argues that the Bible’s language of creation transcends the scientific worldview and provides a comprehensive framework for interpreting reality.  He demonstrates how to read Scripture through this symbolic lens and suggests that adopting this perspective can renew the modern mind by reconnecting it to a spiritual vision of the cosmos.

**Thesis statement(s):** The language of creation offers a unified symbolic model that transcends materialism and invites a renewed engagement with Scripture.

**Commentary:** The conclusion challenges readers to see beyond the limitations of empirical science and to embrace a holistic understanding of the universe.  It suggests that meaning is not imposed on the world but inherent in its structure.

**Skeptical challenge:** Critics may argue that a symbolic model cannot replace scientific explanation and risk fostering anti-scientific attitudes.  A balanced dialogue between science and spirituality may be more fruitful.

**Applications & implications:** If embraced, this model could influence theology, philosophy, pedagogy and personal spirituality, encouraging readers to integrate symbolic awareness into their worldview.
